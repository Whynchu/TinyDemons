# Presentation and Player-Experience Brief

Astra should review the actual player experience, not only the codebase.

## Evaluate

- First-minute clarity: does a new player understand movement, attack, magic, target, guard, roll, Chroma, and goals?
- Hand-feel: do attacks, hitstop, knockback, rolls, casts, damage, and pickups feel immediate and rewarding?
- Combat readability: can the player distinguish enemy roles, attack timing, danger, target priority, and elemental reactions at 240×160?
- Room rhythm: does the game alternate pressure, recovery, discovery, choice, and reward, or become repeated combat corridors?
- Elemental meaning: do elements change decisions and play style, or mainly recolor existing actions?
- Puzzle quality: are puzzles understandable, optional where appropriate, and satisfying without becoming repetitive chores?
- Progression: are Souls, Chroma, Gold, gear, stats, mastery, and rewards understandable and worth pursuing?
- Biome identity: do environments create different moods, enemy patterns, hazards, and tactical questions?
- Touch controls: are controls reachable, separated, readable, and comfortable on a small phone?
- Presentation: does the minimalist style communicate confidence, or expose missing animation, audio, visual hierarchy, or feedback?
- Performance: where would a low-end Android player experience frame drops, input latency, memory pressure, or excessive battery cost?

## Required critique discipline

Separate:

- unfinished implementation from intentional minimalism;
- player friction from personal taste;
- missing content from unnecessary scope;
- architectural risk from ordinary technical debt;
- a strong recommendation from an optional idea.
