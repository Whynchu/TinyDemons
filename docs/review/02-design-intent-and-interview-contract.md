# Design Intent and Interview Contract

Primary source: `../design-interview-record-2026-09-18.md`.

## Identity

- Fighter-first fantasy; exploration supports combat rather than replacing it.
- Cozy and mysterious tone.
- Death should sting lightly; success should feel delightful.
- “Massively tiny” means high variety and expressiveness per rule, not a large feature count.
- Minimalist presentation should feel intentional, readable, and juicy—not unfinished.

## Core design commitments

- Elements behave like classes/loadouts, not merely puzzle colors.
- The player can begin with red, blue, or yellow and acquire other elements through play.
- Each elemental identity should have distinct hand-feel without becoming a mandatory matchup gate.
- The intended loadout has regular magic, held magic, held attack, and elemental passives.
- Mastery should unlock cross-element use rather than create permanent blockers.
- Elemental solutions should usually be optional advantages or alternate routes.
- Every room should ask a timing, positioning, combat, exploration, or decision question.
- Enemy roles should remain behaviorally clean: melee, ranged, healer, airborne.
- Slimey Depths is a normal early biome, not a forced water tutorial.

## Ratified first vertical slice

- Slimey Depths.
- Three starters.
- Biter, Spitter, Healer, and Bat roles.
- Single-role and mixed encounters.
- One optional elemental interaction.
- One non-elemental solution.
- One orb section.
- One key/switch interaction.
- One reward choice.
- One elite or boss.

## Scope and economy commitments

- Five authored floors, generated floors 6–10 per biome, then deeper repeatable content.
- Normal sessions should generally be 2–5 minutes; challenge runs may be 10–20 minutes.
- Souls support binding and skill growth; Chroma is run-local; Gold economy remains to be tuned.
- No pay-to-win.
- Multiplayer, servers, and base-building are deferred dreams, not current scope.

## Open evidence questions

- Gold/Souls market split.
- Stub-gear price and Gold curve.
- Mastery time-to-master.
- Readability with 8–9 actors at 240×160.
- Whether elemental reactions remain visually distinct at the native resolution.
